THE BOARD

§ 1

“So should we do hexagons — maybe we separate each island into 20 hexagons… and the whole ocean will be hexagons, right, just like the land.”

1.1Everything is hexagons

Land and sea are both hex grids. One hexagon is the unit of distance for every rule in this packet.

1.2Islands are 20 hexagons

Each island is made of exactly 20 land hexes. Every player gets one island, and starts with a castle on it.

1.3The ocean between

Ocean hexes fill the space between islands. Boats move on them; soldiers cannot enter them without a transport (§ 4).

1.4Face-down exploring

Ocean and unclaimed land tiles start face down and flip when someone reaches them. Nobody knows the whole map at the start.

1.5Built like a blueprint

The board is layered: the island underneath, the people and buildings on top of it.

Left open How many islands, and how far apart?
Side answer One island per player, arranged around a shared ocean. Leave 4–6 ocean hexes between neighbours: far enough that invading is a real decision, close enough that a 10-hex boat move (§ 4.1) can cross in one go.
Side answer Put 2–3 small unclaimed islands (5 hexes each) in the middle. They give the fleet somewhere to go besides straight at each other, and they are what makes exploring worth doing.
Side answer Hex sizes: cut them about 1.5 inches across. Big enough to stand several soldiers on, small enough that 20 of them fit a poster board.

YOUR TURN

§ 2

“You have two move tokens, one build token and one attack token.” · “Tokens are for moving and placing stuff — and then the attacking is where you roll the dice.”

2.1Four tokens a turn

Every turn you get the same four tokens. Spend them in any order, then your turn ends. Unspent tokens do not carry over.

TokenWhat it buys
Move ×2Move a group up to 5 land hexes, or a fleet up to 10 ocean hexes
Build ×1Place one building, or upgrade one building
Attack ×1Start one fight — the fight itself is settled with dice

2.2Tokens move, dice decide

This is the heart of the game. Tokens are for moving and placing. Once a fight starts, tokens stop mattering and the dice take over (§ 5).

2.3Distance costs time

Because one move token only carries you 5 hexes, crossing your own island takes real turns. That delay is deliberate — it is what makes far-away land feel far away.

Left open Who goes first, and how do you collect resources?
Side answer Everyone rolls one die at the start; highest goes first, then play clockwise. Re-roll ties.
Side answer Add a free Harvest step at the start of each turn, before you spend tokens: collect resources from every hex you own. It costs no token, so gathering never competes with moving.
Side answer Keep the token count at four for the first playtest even if turns feel short. It is far easier to add a fifth token later than to take one away once you are used to it.

MOVING ARMIES

§ 3

“It would be painful to move the whole army one person at a time.” · “Over four people it takes two move tokens to move five spaces of that whole army — technically more efficient than moving one at a time.”

3.1Five hexes per move token

One move token moves a unit up to 5 hexes, in any direction, including diagonally. Somewhere 6 hexes away cannot be reached with a single token, no matter what.

3.2Small groups: up to four

Up to 4 soldiers standing together move as one group for one move token.

3.3Big armies: five or more

An army of 5 or more costs both move tokens — but the whole army moves at once, however big it is. With 20 or 30 soldiers this is enormously better than shuffling them one at a time.

Why this rule is good

It solves the boring problem (moving a big army piece by piece) and creates an interesting one (a big army eats your whole turn, so it cannot move and split up in the same turn). Keep it.

3.4Siege machines march like soldiers

Siege machines move exactly as soldiers do, and count toward the size of the group.

Left open Is there a limit on army size?
Side answer Cap it at 30 soldiers per player — the number you said out loud. It also caps how many pieces you have to make.
Side answer Balloons from the video game fit here neatly: a balloon spends one move token for 5 hexes, flies over walls, water and enemies alike, and carries a single soldier. Only archers and towers can shoot one down.
Side answer A group has to start the move together on one hex. Otherwise a token could sweep up soldiers from all over the island, and the 5-hex limit would stop meaning anything.

BOATS & TRANSPORT

§ 4

“Boats move ten spaces for one move token, because the whole ocean is bigger than one island — and boats move faster than soldiers do.”

4.1Ten hexes per move token

A boat moves up to 10 ocean hexes for one move token — double a soldier, because the ocean is wide.

4.2Fleets follow the army rule

Up to 4 boats move for one token. 5 or more boats cost both move tokens and sail together as a fleet.

4.3Transport boats are special

Ordinary boats cannot carry anyone. Moving soldiers or siege machines across water requires a transport boat, built for that job.

4.4The dock

Each player has one dock area beside the board. Soldiers being shipped are lifted off the island and placed in the dock — they are at sea, so they cannot defend your island or be attacked on it. Leaving them standing on land while also sailing would be an unfair double.

4.5Five aboard, three boats

5 soldiers per transport boat, and at most 3 transport boats at once — so 15 soldiers can be at sea at any moment.

4.6Landing

When a transport reaches its destination, take those soldiers out of the dock and place them where they land.

Left open Does unloading cost a card, or is it free?
Side answer Free. If the transport finishes its move on an ocean hex touching land, the soldiers step off onto that hex immediately. An extra card here would add a rule to remember without adding a decision to make.
Left open What happens if a loaded transport is sunk?
Side answer Everyone aboard is lost — take all 5 out of your dock. Brutal, and exactly right: it turns an invasion into a gamble and gives war boats a job worth doing, guarding the transports.
Your question “Should there be fishing boats?”
Side answer Yes — and make them cheap. A fishing boat parked on an ocean hex next to your island earns 1 food per turn and needs no token to stay there. It gives the sea a peaceful use, and gives raiders something to burn.

BUILDING

§ 5

“So we're going to have the wall, the outpost, the barracks — and how should the armoury work? Something like an armoury shop.”

5.1Builders build where they stand

A builder can only build in the hex it is standing on. To build somewhere far away, you have to walk there first.

5.2What you can build

BuildingWhat it does
CastleYour heart. You start with one; lose it and you are out
WallStops enemies on the ground. They must stop and break it
OutpostA forward base away from the castle
BarracksTrains soldiers
ArmouryA shop — spend gold to make your soldiers stronger
WindmillMust sit next to a farm; turns food into more food
TowerShoots — and is one of only two things that can pop a balloon

5.3Buildings live on paper

Rather than a model for every building, each player keeps a building sheet. Write the building down when you build it, tick it when it is upgraded, cross it out when it is destroyed. There is a printable sheet at the back of this packet.

You disagreed One of you said four buildings per hex, the other said two.
Side answer Two buildings per hex — except your castle's hex, which holds four. Your capital becomes the square worth defending hardest.
Side answer Walls should not count toward that limit. Put them on the edges between hexes instead of inside them — a wall is a line, and on a hex grid an edge is exactly that.
Your question “Should we have upgrades for buildings or not?”
Side answer Yes, but one level only, costing the same again. One tick box per building keeps the paper simple.
Side answer Armoury: pay gold once for +1 on every attack roll, permanently. Simple, expensive, worth racing for.
Side answer Outpost: a builder may build in any hex touching one, not just the hex it stands on. That is how you expand without walking the builder home each time.

FIGHTING & SIEGES

§ 6

“The attacking is where you roll the dice.” · “Siege machines aren't as easy to kill, and they do more damage to walls — how are we going to calculate damage to walls?”

6.1Attacks are dice

Spend your attack token to start a fight between two touching hexes. Both sides roll; the dice decide. Tokens got you there — they do not decide who wins.

6.2Siege machines

  • Harder to kill than a soldier
  • Do far more damage to walls
  • Need a transport boat to cross water (§ 4.3)

6.3Balloons go over

Straight from the video game, and worth protecting: balloons fly over walls. Only archers and towers can shoot one down. A wall is not safety if the sky is open.

The one big gap

Wall damage was the question you ran out of time on. A worked-out answer is in the margin — try it as written for one game, then change the numbers rather than the system.

Your question “How are we going to calculate damage to walls?”
Side answer Give every wall 3 blocks of health, drawn as three tick boxes on your building sheet. Then, spending one attack token, roll one die per attacker:
Rolling on a wall
Soldier5–6 → 1 block
Siege3–6 → 2 blocks
Balloonflies over — no roll
Cross out blocks as they break. At zero the wall is gone and the hex is open.
Why these numbers A lone soldier needs roughly six turns to chew through a wall — hopeless, which is the point. One siege machine does it in about two. That is what makes siege machines worth building and walls worth having.
Side answer Soldier against soldier: both roll one die, higher roll wins and the loser is removed, defender wins ties. Add +2 to the defender if they are behind a standing wall.

7.2Beans are the money

Dried beans as tokens — four kinds, or four colours of paper square. Cheap, and you already have them.

7.3Four kingdoms

Kingdom, Royal, Dark and Forest, the same four as the video game. Give each a small advantage so choosing matters.

KingdomAdvantage
RoyalStarts with extra gold
ForestFarms give +1 food
DarkSoldiers cost 1 less
KingdomRepairs walls for free
Not yet decided How does anybody actually win?
Side answer Pick one before the first game rather than leaving it open — a game with no ending gets abandoned rather than finished.
Conquest Destroy every other castle. The clearest, and the longest.
The Wonder First to build the Grand Cathedral, at an enormous price in all four resources. Lets a peaceful player win, and gives everyone else a clock they can see ticking.
Side answer For game one, use both: first to take every castle or finish the Wonder. Two ways to win means two ways to play, and you will learn twice as much from one evening.
Side answer Harvest rate to start with: 1 resource per matching hex per turn. Round numbers are easy to fix later.

§ 9

SHOPPING LIST

  • Poster board or foam board — the islands
  • Dried beans, four kinds — resources
  • Two dice
  • Small tokens for soldiers: LEGO, buttons or coins, in four colours
  • Coloured paper — four kingdoms
  • Scissors, glue, ruler, pencil
  • The highlighter you already found
  • A folder or box to keep it all in

BUILD IN THIS ORDER

  • Cut 40 hexes and lay out two islands of 20
  • Cut 20 ocean hexes to go between them
  • Make 30 soldiers in two colours
  • Print two building sheets and two turn cards
  • Play the tiny version — castles, soldiers, walls, one way to win. Nothing else
  • Then add boats and transports
  • Then add siege machines and balloons
  • Then add the four kingdoms and the armoury
  • Draw the cover, and ask Dad to enhance it
Side answer Play the tiny version before anything is finished. Most board games die because the makers built every piece first and only then found out that a rule was no fun.
