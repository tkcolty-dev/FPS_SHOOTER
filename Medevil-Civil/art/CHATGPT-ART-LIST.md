# What to ask ChatGPT to draw

Everything Medieval Civilization needs, in the order worth making it.
Generate → save the PNG → drop it into **The Press** → set the size → print.

---

## The one rule that matters most

**Never ask for the whole board as one picture.** ChatGPT makes images about
1024 pixels across. Stretched to a 20-inch board that is roughly **50 dpi** —
a blurry mess. That same 1024-pixel image printed as a **1.5-inch hex** is
about **1000 dpi**, which is sharper than anything your printer can even show.

So: build the map out of **small pieces printed many times over**. The Press
packs 30 hexes onto a sheet, so 80 hexes is only 3 pieces of paper.

## The three habits that keep it all looking like one game

1. **Paste the style block below into every single prompt.** This is the whole
   trick to a matching set. Skip it once and that piece will look adopted.
2. **Ask for one thing per image**, on plain white. Not a sheet of six.
3. After the first image you like, add *"match the style of the last image
   exactly"* to every prompt after it.

## The style block — paste this into every prompt

> Top-down flat view, hand-painted medieval fantasy map art, like an old
> illuminated atlas. Muted parchment palette with deep greens, slate grey,
> warm stone and antique gold. Soft painted texture, bold readable shapes.
> No text, no labels, no numbers, no letters. No border, no frame, no outline
> ring, no drop shadow. Plain pure white background. Square image, subject
> centered and filling the frame.

# 1 · Terrain hexes — make these first

The map is made of these. Ask for the art to **fill a pointy-top hexagon**, so
the shape of the painting itself shows you where to cut. No cut lines needed.

Add to each prompt:

> The art fills a pointy-top hexagon that touches all six edges of the frame,
> with crisp clean edges against the white so it can be cut out. The four
> corners outside the hexagon are pure white.

# 2 · Unit pieces — make these second

These are the things you push around. Small and simple beats detailed here:
at three quarters of an inch, a fussy drawing turns to mush.

Add to each prompt:

> Simple bold silhouette-style piece, seen from directly above at a slight
> forward tilt so it is recognisable. Plain pure white background.

**Colour trick that saves you 32 prompts:** make each unit **once, in grey
stone tones**, then print the sheet on four colours of paper — one per
kingdom. Cheaper, faster, and they will match perfectly.

closely at background.

## Order of work

1. The **seven terrain hexes**. Print them, cut them, lay out one island.
   Stop and look at it. If the map feels good, everything after is easy.
2. **Soldiers and builders** — enough to actually play the tiny version.
3. Everything else, only after you have played once.

## When it comes out of the printer

- Print the **test ruler** in The Press first and measure it. If the line is
  not exactly 6 inches, turn off *Fit to page* in the print box.
- Glue the sheets to cereal-box card before cutting. Pieces that flop over are
  annoying by turn three.
- Cut one hex first and put it on the board next to a soldier. If the soldier
  looks silly on it, change the hex size **now**, before you cut 149 more.
